# Concept Rationales — Refined Board

The active comparison board is `concept-board-v2.svg`. The unversioned board is retained only as a round-one audit artifact.

## 01 — Countercut

A broad curved `כ` controls the silhouette while the full three-part `א` is carved from the right-hand mass. This is the most premium/editorial architecture because the discovery happens inside a quiet outer form. Risk: if the counter is cut all the way to the boundary, the mark fragments; the finalist study therefore contains it within the silhouette.

## 02 — Carved Hinge

The dominant diagonal and upper branch are negative while the lower branch returns as positive mass. The alternating polarity makes the fusion intellectually active. Risk: the polarity switch costs clarity at thumbnail size and feels busier than the brand needs.

## 03 — Continuous Fold

A single rounded `כ` band intersects a broad positive aleph fold. It is fluid, memorable, and easy to animate later without depending on motion. Hard-reject issue: at thumbnail size the positive crossing still reads too readily as a Latin `X`, so this direction was removed from the final three after rendering.

## 04 — Open Planes

Three architectural planes construct the `כ`; negative cuts form the `א`. It has strong technological precision and a systems-engineering tone. Risk: the long cuts reach the outer edge and make the symbol look fragmented.

## 05 — Monolith

A compact rectangular `כ` houses a negative `א`. It is the most favicon-oriented and has strong small-size mass. Its finalist study changes the rectangle into a controlled angular chamber to add ownership and reduce generic app-icon character.

## 06 — Soft Counter

A low, softly curved `כ` holds a tapered negative aleph. The approach is approachable and advisory rather than hard-tech. Risk: the white counter removes too much of the right-hand shoulder, so the large-size silhouette lacks authority.

## 07 — Three-way Junction

The aleph diagonal and branches meet at one white diamond while the kaf shoulders remain positive. It expresses decision-making and convergence. Hard-reject issue: the positive diagonals still create a Latin `X/K` reading before the Hebrew reading appears.

## 08 — Compact Cut

A heavy compact kaf combines internal cuts with a positive central lock. It has strong icon potential and a credible product-technology tone. Risk: the black island inside the white junction adds a fourth reading and becomes noisy below 32 px.

## 09 — Low Horizon

A horizontally compressed kaf holds the aleph in counterform. It is designed to sit efficiently beside a long English name and performs best at 16–24 px. The contained finalist study is the clearest digital-first alternative, though it is less editorial than 01.

## 10 — Tall Aperture

A tall, narrow kaf creates a more fashion/editorial proportion while the aleph is cut through the right mass. Risk: the tallness exaggerates category drift and the counter becomes too delicate in a square avatar.

## 11 — Paired Masses

Top and bottom kaf shoulders operate as separate planes around a locked central counter. It communicates systems and modularity. Risk: the outer silhouette does not hold together quickly enough, and the positive central lock looks decorative.

## 12 — Modular Gate

Rectangular top, bottom, and right bars make a strict engineered kaf; counter cuts form the aleph and a central positive joint. It is the most overtly technological option. Risk: it trends toward cybersecurity/infrastructure branding and loses premium restraint.

## Shortlist

- **A / 01 — Countercut:** strongest premium authority and best abstract-first reading
- **B / 09 — Low Horizon:** strongest small-size and lockup efficiency
- **C / 05 — Monolith:** strongest technological precision and favicon silhouette

The shortlist studies move the aleph cuts away from the outer boundary so each symbol remains a single coherent mass.
